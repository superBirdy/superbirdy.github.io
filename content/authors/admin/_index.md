## About Me

I am an **applied economist working on energy, the environment, agriculture, and policy**, and an Associate Research Scientist in the Department of Agricultural Economics at Texas A&M University, where I received my Ph.D. in Agricultural Economics in 2024.

My research studies how energy and agricultural markets respond to extreme weather, technological change, and public policy, and who bears the costs. I focus on the points where physical systems and markets meet: when a storm disrupts supply, when a new low-carbon or bio-based technology competes with incumbents, or when a policy target asks investors to change course. For example, I trace how Gulf hurricanes move through U.S. fuel markets, how forest loss shifts crop yields and prices far away, how national electricity targets compare with past investment, and what limits the market penetration of bio-based products. My work uses a mixed-methods approach that combines applied econometrics, machine learning, optimization-based market modeling, and life cycle and techno-economic assessment, linking evidence on how markets have behaved to models of how they may respond to new shocks. My goal is to understand how these markets adjust, so that decisions about energy, technology, and the environment rest on how markets actually behave.
